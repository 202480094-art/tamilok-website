Facts About Tamilok

Discover trivia about tamilok you never know.

Misleading Name

Despite often being called a "woodworm," tamilok isn’t a worm at all! It’s actually a type of mollusk, similar to oysters and clams. Its long,
worm-like appearance comes from its elongated, soft body, which allows it to burrow into wood.

Found in Mangroves

Tamilok is found primarily in mangrove forests, particularly in the Philippines and other parts of Southeast Asia. It burrows into the mangrove wood, feeding on the wood’s particles and creating tunnels within.

Unique Taste

Tamilok has a taste often described as a cross between oysters and mussels, with a creamy and slightly salty flavor. Locals enjoy it fresh, marinated with vinegar and spices, or even raw with a dash of calamansi juice!

Nutritional Value

Tamilok is packed with protein, making it a good source of nutrition for people in regions where it’s consumed. It’s also low in fat and high in other minerals, contributing to its growing reputation as an exotic but healthy delicacy.

Cultural Delicacy

In the Philippines, tamilok is considered a delicacy, especially in Palawan. It’s often a dare or a fun experience for tourists to try it, and it’s even celebrated in local festivals, showcasing the unique culinary heritage of the area.
